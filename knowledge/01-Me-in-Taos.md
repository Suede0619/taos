## Health and body: what the ski pass has to respect

From BoulderCentre ortho records, July and August 2025:

- **Right knee:** osteoarthritis plus meniscus tear, pain on the inside near the MCL. Steroid injection Aug 2025. PT referral. Felt "pretty good" on a 3-mile hike afterward.
- **Right hip:** roughly half the cartilage left. Replacement eventually; advice is to wait as long as possible.
- **Spine:** L2 compression fracture (found July 2025) and T4 compression fracture (2019 fall on ice). Degenerative disc disease L4-L5. Sacral low-back pain since a trail fall in September 2024. Worse sitting, better with exercise.
- **Bone density:** two compression fractures at my age means a DEXA scan was ordered. No result on file. **This is the single most important medical follow-up before snowboarding.** Falls are the main risk on a board, and Taos is steep.
- **Other:** Lyme (treated), strong mold sensitivity (test the house), grass pollen allergy, cataract lens implants, history of depression. Never smoked. BMI 24.4. I exercise 1 to 3 hours a day: swimming, hiking, weights, yoga, cold plunges.

**Implications**
- Snowboard with a back protector, impact shorts, wrist guards, and a helmet. Ride groomers and soft snow days early season. Skip Kachina and the hike-to terrain until the body says yes. Weekday pass keeps me off crowded weekends, which is also safer.
- Qi gong and tai chi are the perfect complement: low impact, balance, hip mobility, spinal awareness. This isn't a hobby, it's the physical therapy.
- Get a primary care doctor and a PT in Taos in the first month. Transfer records from BoulderCentre. Ask for the DEXA result or redo it at Holy Cross.
- Swimming is my best joint-safe cardio. Find the pool.
- Altitude: Arroyo Seco is about 7,600 ft, the ski valley base 9,200 ft, Kachina 12,481 ft. Boulder was 5,400 ft. Give it two weeks before hard efforts. Hydrate; osha tea is the local altitude remedy (see [[04-Body-Energy-Qigong-TCM]]).

## The Taos hypothesis

Buena Vista failed on "too small, no gay community, all homeowners." Taos is also small, but it has: a Hanuman temple with a daily free lunch, a world-class steep mountain 12 miles up the road, a deep arts and pottery culture, a real AA community, hot springs, Santa Fe within reach, and a long tradition of eccentrics, seekers and queers. The experiment is: **does a life built on service, practice, craft, and mountains produce the friendships and the partner that chasing them directly never did?** One year to find out. The lease says so.
