# Mushrooms: gourmet, medicinal, and the New Mexico psilocybin program

*Researched 2026-09-28. What carries over from MycoWeb: the 50-state-legal core (gourmet, functional extracts, grow kits, genetics library, education) and the $340K lean model. What does not: everything tied to Colorado's Natural Medicine Health Act. And the founding rule stands: no interstate transfer of regulated material, ever.*

## Start small in the house: a winter grow

The realistic Nov-May plan is indoor grow tents in my room or a shed, not a facility. Ask Suzie about a shed, garage corner or outbuilding, and about humidity in the house (mold sensitivity: keep the tent sealed and vented outdoors, run a HEPA filter, and never fruit in the bedroom).

- **Species for a first winter:** blue and pink oyster (fast, forgiving), lion's mane (the medicinal-gourmet crossover), shiitake on supplemented sawdust blocks. Reishi and turkey tail for extracts in spring.
- **Setup:** one 2x4 grow tent, ultrasonic humidifier, small inline fan, LED strip, pressure cooker or the "all-in-one bag" route to skip sterilization at first. Roughly $300-500.
- **Spawn and supplies: New Mexico Fungi, 3540 Pan American Fwy NE Ste D, Albuquerque, (505) 387-9840**, 15 rotating species including lion's mane and oysters. https://www.nmfungi.com/ Call for spawn and culture availability. Order online otherwise (Field & Forest, North Spore).
- **Regional growers to learn from:** FunG Farms (Santa Fe; oysters, lion's mane, reishi, wine caps; Eldorado market Fridays 3-6), Desert Fungi (north of Santa Fe, since 2000), Full Circle Mushrooms (La Mesa). Visit FunG Farms on a Santa Fe day and ask about mentoring.
- High-desert reality: 7,600 ft, very dry, cold nights. Humidity control is the whole game. Substrate and water are cheap; the tent is the facility.

## Selling (2027)

- **Taos Farmers Market**, courthouse lot, 105 Albright St. Saturdays 8-12:30 May through October, plus three November markets. **Non-farmer applications due April 1**; returning vendors reapply yearly. One local mushroom vendor already sells there (find them, learn from them, don't compete on the same table). info@taosfarmersmarket.org, http://taosfarmersmarket.org/vendors/
- **Taos Winter Market Gathering**, Saturdays 10-2, mid-Nov to early April, at Taos Cow @ Anglada's, 736 Kit Carson Rd. Free, artisans and produce. 2026-27 dates pending. Vendor inquiries: Diane Dau, Taoswmg@gmail.com. A winter table for dried lion's mane and grow kits is plausible.
- **NM Homemade Food Act (HB 177, 2021):** no permit for non-hazardous home-processed foods sold direct to consumers. Dried mushrooms most plausibly fall under "dried fruits and vegetables" but are not named; **confirm with NMED Food Program (505) 827-2855.** Needs a food-handler card and a full label. Not covered: tinctures, wholesale, online. Fresh whole mushrooms sold by the grower are raw agricultural products. Fact sheet: https://www.newmexicofma.org/docs/FINAL_-_2024-Homemade-Food-Act.pdf
- Restaurant channel: Aceq and Sol Food in Seco, the temple kitchen (as an offering), Taos restaurants. Lion's mane sells itself to chefs.

## NM Medical Psilocybin Act (SB 219), status September 2026

- Signed April 2025, effective June 20, 2025, NMSA 26-2D. Qualifying conditions: treatment-resistant depression, PTSD, substance use disorders, end-of-life care (board may add). Natural psilocybin only. Required preparation, dosing and integration sessions. **No home cultivation or personal-use provision; wild collection remains illegal.**
- **Rule 7.35.2 NMAC (Producer and Laboratory Requirements) effective June 23, 2026.** DOH opened **producer permit applications Aug 17, 2026**, no deadline, reviewed in order received. Requires: NM business license, SOS and Tax & Rev registration, certificate of occupancy, fire, electrical and HVAC inspections, zoning, proof of ownership or landlord acknowledgment, attestation that all product stays in NM, employee food-safety certificates. Producers sell only to other producers and licensed practitioners; state traceability; every lot lab-tested. One ownership interest per person; non-transferable. Fees not found. An attorney told Source NM to expect "only a small number of highly qualified applications."
- Timeline: statute requires implementation by Dec 31, 2027; DOH aims to enroll first patients by end of December 2026, with lab and clinician permits after. Advisory board (9 seats) met Dec 5, 2025 and Jan 30, 2026; public hearing April 24, 2026.
- Sources: https://www.nmhealth.org/about/mcpp/mpp/ , https://www.nmhealth.org/about/mcpp/mpp/mpab/ , https://sourcenm.com/2026/08/27/new-mexico-psilocybin-program-opens-permitting-applications-for-psychedelic-mushroom-producers/
- **Follow:** DOH E-News (Medical Psilocybin list) at nmhealth.org; medical.psilocybin@doh.nm.gov, 505-827-2321.
- **My read:** this is a commercial-facility license with no small-grower tier, and it's a medical model, not Colorado's healing-center model. The MycoWeb thesis ("be the infrastructure under every retreat center") has a New Mexico version: the **testing lab** and the **genetics and education layer**. But a Taos-based producer application needs a compliant building, capital, and a partner. Year one here is for learning the program, meeting the players, and growing legal mushrooms well. Rewrite the MycoWeb legal brief for NM in spring if the pull is real.
