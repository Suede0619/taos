## 6. What the deed situation most likely is (inference)

A deed vesting title in Oliver Dyer-Bennet, who died in 2024, was recorded 02/17/2026. A deed to a grantee who is already dead when it is signed is void, so either (a) Susan signed and delivered the deed to Oliver before his death (as security for a private loan, or as an outright transfer with an understanding she could stay), and the executor found it in Oliver's papers and recorded it in 2026; or (b) the grantor was someone else entirely (for example Stan's heirs, if Stan's 2006 probate distributed an interest in the tract); or (c) it is a court instrument (unlikely; no case exists). Susan continuing to pay a $1,745/month mortgage on a house she no longer holds record title to fits scenario (a) with a private loan. Under Bell v. Ware, 69 N.M. 308 (1961), a deed absolute in form can be shown to be a mortgage, but only by clear and convincing evidence in a lawsuit.

The signing/notary date on document #492376, the grantor's name, the stated consideration, and the document type resolve this.

## 8. NM law, short version (see agent memo for citations)

- UORRA "owner" (§47-8-3) includes anyone with beneficial ownership and a right to present use, mortgagees in possession, and lessors. Susan very likely qualifies as your "owner" under the Act; the lease is valid between you and her.
- A lease binds only the interest the lessor has. It does not bind the Dyer-Bennet estate.
- Nobody in peaceable residential possession can be removed without court process (§47-8-40 et seq.; §35-10-1). Floor timeline from a hostile PR: 30-day notice, petition, trial 7-10 days after service, writ 3-7 days after judgment; about 6-8 weeks minimum, longer if contested. Susan would contest. Police treat it as civil when the occupant shows a lease, receipts, and ID at the address. Criminal trespass (§30-14-1) requires knowing that consent was withdrawn "by the owner or occupant"; the occupant's consent is a defense.
- Only an appointed PR with NM authority (ancillary filing under §45-4-204/205, or a local ancillary administration) can act on NM real property. None is on file.
- Foreclosure of the 2003 mortgage would wipe a 2026 lease, but the federal PTFA gives a bona fide tenant 90 days' notice minimum; a written lease at market rent with receipts is what proves bona fides.
- Deposit: one month max for terms under a year; returned with itemization within 30 days (§47-8-18). Zelle payments are not reversible; Reg E covers only unauthorized transfers.
- Help: NM Legal Aid 1-833-545-4357 (Taos office 204 Montoya St); NM Courts HelpLine 855-268-7804; Taos Magistrate Court 575-758-4030 (small claims to $10,000, $77 filing).

### What this does to the quitclaim deed
Susan's quitclaim to "Oliver Dyer-Bennet" is dated **06/18/2025, six months after Oliver died** and about two months after John was (presumably) appointed PR in Sonoma. A deed to a grantee who is dead when the deed is executed has no grantee capable of taking title and is generally void; it does not pass title to the decedent's estate or heirs. (General common-law rule; verify with a NM real estate attorney; no NM case pulled.) Consequences:
1. Record title is clouded in favor of a dead man, but **legal title most likely never left Susan**.
2. The estate cannot sell or insure the house on this deed; it would need Susan to sign a corrective deed to "John Dyer-Bennet, Personal Representative of the Estate of Oliver Dyer-Bennet," or sue in NM to quiet/reform title. John's "tell Susie to call me, we can work something out" is consistent with needing her signature.
3. Whether Susan owes the estate anything (a loan from Oliver, an unrecorded contract, a promise) is a separate question the deed does not answer. The Sonoma probate inventory (Form DE-160) and any creditor claims would show if the estate lists the NM house or a debt from Susan as an asset.
