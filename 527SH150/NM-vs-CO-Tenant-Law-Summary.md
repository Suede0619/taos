# New Mexico vs. Colorado tenant law: what changes for me

*Written 2026-09-28 for a room rental in an owner-occupied house in Taos County. Not legal advice. New Mexico's landlord-tenant statute is the Uniform Owner-Resident Relations Act ("UORRA" or "the Act"), NMSA 1978, Sections 47-8-1 through 47-8-52. Read the text at nmonesource.com. Verify anything you plan to rely on with New Mexico Legal Aid (lawhelpnewmexico.org, 1-833-545-4357) or the NM Attorney General's Renters' Guide.*

## The short version

New Mexico is a faster, thinner, more landlord-leaning state than Colorado. Colorado spent 2019 to 2024 building tenant protections (expanded warranty of habitability, for-cause eviction, late-fee caps, rent-increase limits, 10-day nonpayment notice). New Mexico's Act dates mostly from 1975 and 1995 and has not had that wave. The practical consequences:

1. **Evictions move fast.** Three-day notice for nonpayment, seven days for other breaches, court hearing within about ten days.
2. **No just-cause protection.** After the fixed term, the landlord can end a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days' notice for any reason or none.
3. **Habitability exists in the statute, but the remedies are narrower.** No repair-and-deduct. The tool is a seven-day written notice, then rent abatement or termination.
4. **The lease matters more.** Because the statute is thin, what's written in the agreement carries more of the weight. That's why the lease I drafted spells out entry, deposit, early termination and rent.

## Does the Act apply to a room in the owner's house?

Yes, as far as I can tell. Section 47-8-9 lists the exclusions (institutions, contracts of sale, fraternal organizations, transient hotel stays, employee housing) and does not exclude a room rented in an owner-occupied home. So the Act's deposit, entry, habitability and eviction rules all apply to this lease. In Colorado the same was true. Neither Taos nor Taos County has a local rental ordinance like Boulder's rental licensing or its renter-protection code (the EPRAS-type stuff I researched for SafeSpace does not exist here).

## Warranty of habitability

**Colorado:** a named statutory warranty (C.R.S. 38-12-503), expanded in 2019 and 2024, with a long list of conditions (mold, pests, heat, hot water, and more), tight response deadlines, rent withholding into escrow, repair-and-deduct, and the right to break the lease.

**New Mexico:** there is no statute titled "warranty of habitability," but **Section 47-8-20** imposes the same duties on the owner: comply with building and housing codes that materially affect health and safety, make repairs and keep the premises in a safe condition, keep common areas safe and clean, maintain electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating and ventilating systems and any appliances the owner supplies, and supply running water, reasonable hot water and reasonable heat. New Mexico courts have also treated residential leases as carrying an implied warranty of habitability, but the statute is the tool you actually use.

**How you enforce it in NM (Sections 47-8-27.1 and 47-8-27.2):**
- Give the owner **written notice** describing the problem.
- If it is not fixed within **seven days**, you may **terminate** on a date you name in the notice, or claim **rent abatement**: roughly one-third of the daily pro-rated rent for each day of an ordinary violation, and 100 percent per day for any period the unit is uninhabitable. Verify the percentages against the statute text before invoking them.
- Keep paying rent unless you are formally abating; don't just withhold.
- There is **no statutory repair-and-deduct** in New Mexico.

For a shared house the realistic habitability issues are heat, hot water, and mold. Given my mold history, test before moving in and put anything you find on Exhibit A.

## Side-by-side

| Topic | New Mexico (UORRA) | Colorado |
|---|---|---|
| Security deposit limit | One month's rent for terms under a year; if more than one month on a lease of a year or longer, owner must pay interest (47-8-18) | No statutory cap |
| Deposit return | **30 days** after termination and move-out, with itemized deductions. Owner who misses this forfeits the right to keep any of it and may owe your attorney fees (47-8-18) | 30 days by default (up to 60 if the lease says); treble damages for bad-faith retention (38-12-103) |
| Late fees | Max **10 percent** of the period's rent; must be in the written lease (47-8-15(D)). Your $50 on $825 is about 6 percent | Max the greater of $50 or 5 percent; 7-day grace period; must be in the lease (38-12-105) |
| Rent increase | Fixed term: no change unless the lease allows. Month to month: **30 days' written notice** before the rent due date (47-8-15(F)) | 60 days' notice; no more than one increase per 12 months |
| Owner entry | **24 hours' notice**, reasonable times, except emergencies (47-8-24) | No state statute; lease terms govern |
| Ending a month-to-month tenancy | **30 days' written notice** by either party before the periodic rental date (47-8-37) | 21 to 91 days depending on tenancy length (13-40-107); plus the 2024 for-cause eviction law limits nonrenewal after 12 months |
| Just-cause eviction | None | Yes (HB24-1098) |
| Nonpayment notice | **3 days** (47-8-33(D)) | 10 days (13-40-104) |
| Other lease breaches | **7-day notice to cure**; a second similar breach within six months can be a 7-day notice with no cure (47-8-33(A), (B)) | 10-day notice to cure for most breaches |
| Repair-and-deduct | No | Yes, within limits |
| Rent abatement | Yes, after 7-day notice (47-8-27.2) | Yes, via escrow procedure |
| Retaliation | Presumed retaliatory if within 6 months of a complaint or exercising rights (47-8-39) | Similar (38-12-509) |
| Lockouts, utility shutoffs | Prohibited; damages available (47-8-36) | Prohibited |
| Owner must disclose | Name and address of owner and anyone authorized to manage (47-8-19); a signed copy of the agreement | Similar, plus more disclosures (bed bugs, etc.) |
| Local rental licensing, just-cause codes | None in Taos | Boulder, Denver and others have them |

## What this means for the lease we wrote

- **The 30-day month-to-month notice is statutory** in NM, so Section 3 of the lease matches the law. Colorado would have required 21 days for a tenancy this length.
- **The 24-hour entry notice is statutory** in NM; Section 9 restates it and adds a lock, which the statute does not require.
- **"No rent increase during the term" is implied** under the Act for a fixed term, because the owner cannot change the terms of a written fixed-term agreement unilaterally. It is written in anyway because it costs nothing and removes an argument. During month-to-month, the lease follows the 30-day rule.
- **The deposit clause tracks 47-8-18** (30 days, itemized). The $200 deposit is well under the one-month cap. Prepaid last month's rent is not a "deposit" under the Act, which is why the lease says explicitly which month it pays for and what happens if the tenancy ends early.
- **The early-termination clause is pure contract.** The Act gives no general right to break a fixed term for hardship, so if you want one it has to be in the lease. On a six-month term at $825, the most you could owe by walking out on day one without a clause is about $4,950 minus whatever she mitigates by re-renting (NM owners have a duty to mitigate). So the clause is worth having but it is not life-or-death the way it would be on a twelve-month lease.
- **The seven-day habitability notice** is your remedy if heat, hot water or mold fails. Put it in writing, keep a copy, date it.
