# 527 State Hwy 150, Arroyo Seco: analysis

*Written September 30, 2026. Research-only; not legal advice. Sources and URLs for every fact below are in `Research-Report-2026-09-30.md`. Statutes cited are NMSA 1978.*

## Executive summary

**Is this a scam?** No. Susan Marie "Suzie" Nelson (born 1957) is a real, long-term resident of 527 State Hwy 150. The phone she gave is hers. Her REAL ID was issued at the address in March 2025. She holds a mortgage on the house in her own name, originated January 17, 2003 (about $295,000 at 5.875%, serviced by Cenlar), and she has paid it every month through mid-2026 at $1,745 a month. Gas and electric are in her name (the electric account still reads "Nelson Stan R or Susan").

**Does she own the house?** Almost certainly yes, but the county record says otherwise. The Taos County Assessor lists the owner as Oliver Dyer-Bennet of Oakland, California, based on a **quitclaim deed that Susan herself signed on June 18, 2025**, first recorded around January 2026 (Doc. #491726, with no legal description) and re-recorded February 17, 2026 (Doc. #492376, Book 1262 Page 87) with the legal description added. **Oliver Dyer-Bennet died December 13, 2024**, six months before that deed was signed. A deed to a person who is already dead has no grantee and, under the general rule, conveys nothing. So legal title most likely never left Susan, and the record is clouded by a defective document.

**Who is on the other side?** John Dyer-Bennet (about 79, Oakland, (510) 654-2871), Oliver's brother and court-appointed personal representative: Sonoma County Superior Court case 25PR00208 (filed February 27, 2025, attorney Ryan Henderson, Healdsburg) and an ancillary Nevada estate, Douglas County 2025-PB-00081, which petitioned for final distribution in June 2026. **Nothing has been filed in any New Mexico court**, ever, by or against anyone involved. Oliver was a well-known California glider pilot and importer; Stan Nelson (Lt. Col. Stanley F. Nelson, USAF and NASA pilot, glider record-holder, briefly Taos airport manager, died March 12, 2005) was almost certainly his friend from the soaring world. That is the likely origin of whatever arrangement led Susan to sign the deed.

**Can the sheriff show up a week after I move in?** No. Under New Mexico law nobody in peaceable residential possession can be removed without a court process: notice, a petition for restitution, a trial 7 to 10 days after service, and a writ 3 to 7 days after judgment. The floor is six to eight weeks from a first notice, and only after the estate opens a New Mexico proceeding, which it has not done. Susan would contest, which turns it into a district-court title case measured in months. A signed agreement, rent receipts, and the occupant's consent are exactly what keep a renter on the "resident with due process" side of the line rather than the "trespasser" side (§30-14-1 requires knowing that consent was withdrawn "by the owner or occupant").

**What is the real risk?** Medium-term, not immediate. Susan signed away her house on a do-it-yourself form to a dead man, which means either she owed the estate something, or she meant to honor a promise to Oliver, or someone talked her into it. Any of those can resurface as a settlement, a corrective deed, or a sale within the next year. The estate has no leverage unless Susan gives it to them by signing again. If she gets advice first, the tenancy is safe; if she signs a corrective deed without advice, she becomes a guest in her own home and the tenancy depends on the estate.

**Decision.** Move in on the corrected agreement (`03-Room-Rental-Agreement-527-Hwy-150.md`), keep money exposure to one month at a time, get Susan to New Mexico Legal Aid, and check the county index monthly. If a corrective deed, lis pendens, or New Mexico court filing appears, that is the 30-day signal to start looking.

## 3. The deed, and why it probably fails

A quitclaim conveys whatever interest the grantor has to the grantee. It requires a grantee capable of taking. Oliver had been dead six months when Susan signed on June 18, 2025, and John was already the appointed (or nearly appointed) personal representative in California. A lawyer-drafted deed would have named "John Dyer-Bennet, as Personal Representative of the Estate of Oliver Dyer-Bennet." This one named the decedent, on a generic form titled for New Mexico, without a legal description, which was added by re-recording eight months later. Nothing about it was done by the estate's California or Nevada lawyers. (General common-law rule; a New Mexico real estate attorney should confirm; no New Mexico case was pulled.)

Consequences if void: title never left Susan; the record is clouded; a title company would not insure the estate; the estate needs either Susan's signature on a corrective deed or a quiet-title action in Taos District Court. Consequences if a court somehow upheld it: the estate owns the house subject to Susan's mortgage (she stays personally liable on the note), Susan is an occupant at sufferance, and any tenant of hers holds only what she holds.

Separate from the deed's validity is the question of **why** she signed it. If Oliver lent her money and she believed she owed him the house, New Mexico treats a deed given as security as a mortgage (Bell v. Ware, 69 N.M. 308 (1961)), but only by clear and convincing evidence in litigation. If she meant it as a gift to honor a promise, the estate may argue it as evidence of intent. If she did not understand what she was signing, that is a capacity and possible exploitation question.

## 4. The law, as it applies to a room-renter

- **Susan is the "owner" under the Act** (§47-8-3 includes anyone with beneficial ownership and a right to present use, mortgagees in possession, and lessors). The agreement is valid between her and the renter; she owes every duty in the Act (deposit, 24-hour entry, habitability, no lockouts).
- **The agreement does not bind the estate.** A lessor grants only what she has. The renter's protection against a superior title claimant is procedural, not contractual: no removal without the §47-8-40 process or §35-10-1 unlawful detainer.
- **Timeline of a hostile removal:** 30-day notice (month-to-month) or 3 to 7 days (breach) → petition → trial 7 to 10 days after service (§47-8-43) → writ 3 to 7 days after judgment (§47-8-46). Six to eight weeks minimum, uncontested; contested title, months.
- **Police.** New Mexico has no squatter fast-track (HB 332 of 2025 and HB 144 of 2026 both died). Criminal trespass (§30-14-1) requires knowing consent was withdrawn by "the owner or occupant"; the occupant's written consent is a defense.
- **Foreclosure** of the 2003 mortgage would extinguish a 2026 lease, but the federal Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act gives a bona fide tenant 90 days minimum; a written lease at market rent with receipts proves bona fides. Susan is current.
- **Estate authority in New Mexico:** only a personal representative who files in New Mexico (§45-4-204, 45-4-205, or a local ancillary administration) can act on New Mexico real property. None has.
- **Money:** deposit capped at one month for terms under a year, returned with itemization within 30 days (§47-8-18). Zelle is not reversible. Small claims in Taos Magistrate Court to $10,000, $77 filing.
- **Residency:** a signed agreement and rent receipts, not a driver's license, are what establish resident status under the Act.

## 6. What could still go wrong, ranked

1. **Susan signs a corrective deed for John without advice.** Most likely bad outcome; fully preventable. Refer her to Legal Aid now.
2. **The estate files in Taos** (foreign-PR filing plus quiet title or ejectment). Slow, visible in the NM Case Lookup, and gives months of runway.
3. **Susan sells or settles** and a buyer wants the house. Covered by the 30-day and 15-day exits and the refund clause.
4. **Cenlar calls the loan** under a due-on-sale clause after noticing the recorded deed. Unlikely while current; PTFA gives 90 days regardless.
5. **Susan's capacity declines** and someone else takes over her affairs. Watch for it; be kind; the agreement's notice clauses still bind whoever acts for her.
